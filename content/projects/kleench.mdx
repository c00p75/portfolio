  - >-
    Every wallet is three sub-ledgers (available, escrowed and pending) on a double-entry ledger, and a transfer either commits fully or rolls back. The sum of every sub-ledger must always equal a segregated trust account at a licensed bank, one to one, and any mismatch alerts finance.
  - >-
    Marketplace payments sit in escrow until release, when the seller is paid net of the platform fee and 15% withholding tax, which is queued for the revenue authority. Withdrawals park funds in pending, so a failed mobile money payout returns to available instead of vanishing.
  - >-
    Four KYC tiers, from a phone number with a zero balance up to lending and insurance, each with its own documents and balance cap. Audit records are hash-chained so tampering shows.
  - >-

## What it is

A wallet is the part of a commerce app a regulator cares about, so it had to be
explained before it was built. The document walks the Bank of Zambia through the
ledger, escrow, KYC, anti-money-laundering rules and what happens when the
national payment switch goes down: person-to-person and escrow keep working, and
mobile money flows queue until it's back.

## Status

This is a design, not a running system. The Kleench app itself is a prototype
built by another engineer; its wallet screens are interface only, and the money
movement described here is what they would sit on.
