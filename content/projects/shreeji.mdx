## The problem

Shreeji sells computers, power systems and networking hardware in Lusaka, and
has done since 1998. Putting that catalogue online is not the hard part. Two
things about the business are.

The first is currency. Stock is bought in dollars and sold in kwacha, against a
rate that moves, plus 16% VAT. So a price is not a field someone types — it is
the output of a calculation whose inputs change, and every one of those inputs
has to be inspectable later when a customer asks why the number differs from
last week's quote.

The second is that a Zambian business buyer often does not pay with a card. They
pay by bank transfer, or they collect and pay on pickup. Both mean the order
exists and is unpaid for hours or days, which is a state a checkout flow modelled
on card-or-nothing does not have anywhere to put.

## What I built

Both halves: the NestJS API and the Next.js frontend that serves the storefront,
the customer portal and the admin console.

The parts worth reading about are the payment abstraction — one gateway
interface with a card path that is only ever confirmed server-side, an
asynchronous bank-transfer path with a deadline and a job that enforces it, and
a cash-on-pickup path that skips the gateway — and the order transaction, which
locks the stock it is about to spend rather than trusting a read.

Around those: an admin console that owns the catalogue, inventory with stock
valuation, coupons, loyalty, returns and the site's own page copy, so marketing
changes do not need a deploy. Gateway credentials and saved card tokens are
AES-256 encrypted at rest, with only the last four digits of a card stored in
the clear. Product images run through a background-removal service and land in
MinIO rather than the application's filesystem.

Guest checkout can look an order up afterwards without an account, through an
endpoint that 404s when the email does not match the order — an unauthenticated
lookup that answers "wrong email" differently from "no such order" is an email
enumeration oracle.

## What is not finished

Card checkout has not had an end-to-end retest against a real card — DPO's
redirect, 3DS and callback in one pass — because that cannot be automated
without a live card and an OTP. Until it has, I would not call that path
verified, only implemented.

Payment retries and refunds are specified and not built: a declined card is
currently a dead end the customer restarts, and a refund is a manual
reconciliation between the gateway and the order. There is also a scaling
question I have not answered — the API has been intermittently slow on staging
while Postgres itself stayed responsive, which points at the process rather than
the database, and I have not yet instrumented it well enough to say more than
that.
