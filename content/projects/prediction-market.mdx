## Why an automated market maker

A prediction market with no liquidity is not a market, it is a form. An order book
needs traders on both sides before it can quote anything, and a platform launching
new markets continuously never has that on day one.

A scoring-rule market maker always quotes, and its prices move as a function of
the positions held rather than of who happens to be online. The cost is that the
platform itself carries inventory risk, bounded by the liquidity parameter — which
is the trade being made deliberately in exchange for a market that works from the
first trade.

## Why the ledger is double-entry

Because the alternative is a balance column, and a balance column cannot tell you
that it is wrong. In a system taking deposits, charging fees, and paying out
settlements, the useful property is not that the arithmetic is correct — it is
that incorrect arithmetic is *detectable*. Double-entry gives you an invariant to
check rather than a number to trust.
