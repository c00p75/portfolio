## Why an automated market maker

A prediction market with no liquidity isn't a market. It's a form. An order book
needs traders on both sides before it can quote anything, and a platform launching
new markets continuously never has that on day one.

A scoring-rule market maker always quotes, and its prices move as a function of
the positions held rather than of who happens to be online. The cost is that the
platform itself carries inventory risk, bounded by the liquidity parameter. That's
a trade you make on purpose, in exchange for a market that works from the very
first trade.

## Why the ledger is double-entry

Because the alternative is a balance column, and a balance column can't tell you
when it's wrong. In a system taking deposits, charging fees and paying out
settlements, the property you want isn't that the arithmetic is correct. It's that
incorrect arithmetic is *detectable*. Double-entry hands you an invariant to check
instead of a number to trust.
